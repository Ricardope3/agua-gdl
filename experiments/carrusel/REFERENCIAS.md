# Carousels Ricardo admires (looked at 6 Nov 2026)

What each does well and what Agua GDL can borrow. Seen in Chrome; counts as shown that day.

## @observaciudades.tec (Observatorio de Ciudades, Tec de Monterrey)

Looked at: «¿A quiénes afectará la rotación de agua en Guadalajara?» (7 slides, 451 likes, 27 Oct) and the Estadio Guadalajara congestion map (2 slides).

- **The cover is a question the reader wants answered**, set big over a flat illustration (tap, drops, cupped hands) on a faint map. No photo, no number.
- **One fact per slide, always on the same map.** Every slide redraws the rotation polygon over the metro area, then layers one thing: population, schools, hospitals, businesses. Repetition makes the comparison effortless.
- **Big number, small label, tiny donut**: «49%» with a ring filled to 49%, «de las escuelas del AMG».
- **Plain legend on every map** (a colour ramp with its range, «Polígono de rotación»).
- **Ends on a question, not a call to action** («¿Qué posibilidades tienen distintos sectores…?»), drawn with the same illustrations.
- **The caption carries the full argument** with the numbers repeated, so the post reads without swiping.
- **Consistent system**: one sans-serif, black text, a dotted crop frame with corner marks, logo top left, two or three colours per post.

## @tulipandelmonte (a conservation scientist in Nuevo León)

Looked at: «¿Cómo saber si un sitio necesita restauración?» (8 slides, 676 likes in 2 days) and the Topo Chico campaign reel (76.8K likes).

- **A person on camera**: she is in most photos, doing the fieldwork. It reads as her teaching, not an institution.
- **Real photos of the problem, annotated by hand**: arrows, callouts («Inicio de la cárcava»), sticky notes, stars, dashed paths that lead to the next slide.
- **Teaches a skill**: each slide is one thing to look for, so people save it.
- **Born from a follower's question** («En respuesta a una de las preguntas…») and ends asking what they want next; comments become the next post.
- **The campaign that went viral is a clear «NO a…» with one place and one action** (sign the petition, link in bio), on a short emotional video of an animal in the place at risk.

## @roclapaz (Red de Observadores Ciudadanos, La Paz)

Looked at: «¿Detectaste una ilegalidad ambiental?» (5 slides, posted that day).

- **A practical guide**: cover photo of a volunteer pointing at the coast, then numbered steps «1. Datos generales», «2. Detalla los hechos», «3. Identificación y solicitud».
- **Shows the actual document**: a mock complaint form on every step slide, with the part being explained highlighted and the rest faded, and callouts to each field.
- **Ends with safety** («Tu seguridad es primero»: don't confront, don't trespass) over a warm group photo.
- **Brand system**: navy and teal, a thin colour stripe under titles, round icon badges; bilingual caption.
- Other posts: a parrotfish that speaks in first person about its bay, and a directory of authorities to call.

## What to borrow for Agua GDL

1. **Covers ask the reader's question** («¿Te toca corte mañana?», «¿Tu colonia está entre las más altas?»), not our headline.
2. **One map, many layers**: our colonias map with one fact per slide (rotation zones, days without water, elevation, pipas). We have the geometry in `web/colonias_siapa.topo.json`.
3. **How-to guides people save**: how to ask for a pipa, how to ask for the 80% discount, how to write to your diputación, each showing the actual form or message with the part explained highlighted (the ROC pattern).
4. **Annotate real photos** (arrows and callouts on a photo of turbid water, an empty tinaco, a pipa line), without faces.
5. **Answer followers' questions** and say so; end by asking what they want next.
6. **Lighter, warmer look for guides**: a flat-colour or illustrated variant beside the dark-photo one, keeping our drop, blue and fonts.
7. **Caption tells the whole story**, numbers included, for people who don't swipe.
8. **Campaign posts**: one place, one ask, one link.
